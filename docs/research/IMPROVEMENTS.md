# Improving on the open-source tools LightBench uses: classical algorithms and AI models

**Prepared for:** Shawn Burke (LightBench) · **Date:** 2026-10-13 · **Status:** research only. Nothing in the repository was changed.

**The owner's brief (2026-10-13):** a long-running track to improve on the open-source tools LightBench is built on (darktable, Lensfun, LibRaw, RawTherapee ideas, LightCraft), both classical algorithms and AI models. Sources are credited and the improvements stay in LightBench (no upstream contributions planned). The four goals are **image quality (IQ)**, **speed in the browser**, **licence freedom** (our own permissively licensed weights where possible) and **ease of use**. Where two goals conflict heavily, this report says so (marked **CONFLICT**).

## How to read this

- **[read]**: read at the primary source during this research (GitHub raw files, the local darktable checkout at `6271f53`, LightBench's own branches).
- **[code-read]**: a lead found by reading code on both sides. It has **not** been tested by running anything.
- **[doc]**: taken from LightBench's own docs (PHASE*.md, docs/research/*, the new briefs). The doc and line are given.
- **[secondary]**: read in another project's document that quotes the source (e.g. darktable-ai's model cards), not at the source itself.
- **[unconfirmed]**: could not be read from here, or from memory. Blocked from this machine: huggingface.co, arxiv.org, Google Patents, helpx.adobe.com, docs.darktable.org, ai.meta.com, places2.csail.mit.edu, dataverse.uclouvain.be, commons.wikimedia.org, polyhaven.com, the SIDD and DIV2K sites, hdrplusdata.org.
- **[estimate]**: arithmetic or judgement, not a measurement. Every compute figure is an estimate, with its basis.
- Patent and licence facts are reported as facts. **Nothing here is legal advice.**
- Goal effects use: **++** large gain, **+** gain, **0** none, **−** loss.
- Shipped vs unshipped matters for process versions. On `main` (60b1170): PHASE3 Parts 1–11 (crop, lens, Upright, X-Trans, tone equalizer v2, Remove, export sharpening). **Not yet merged:** Part 12 Tone Curve (`origin/lightbench/tone-curve`, 954dde7) and Part 13 Detail (`origin/lightbench/detail`, 6c6b542). Planned, not built: Colour Mixer, Effects, Masking.

**Process-version rule** (PHASE3 Part 1, `engine/develop/process.ts`): a change that alters a saved edit's pixels needs a new process version; a new tool or a new option that is off by default does not. So a fix to an **unmerged** tool costs nothing if it lands before the merge, and the same fix after the merge needs a version (and an "Update to current process" offer). That makes "fix before merge" the cheapest window for the Detail and Tone Curve items below.

## Summary

1. **The cheapest wins are in the two unmerged tools.**
   - **Detail noise reduction** removes 4–9 % more noise than darktable with non-flat curves (Part 13, Verified). Reading both codes gives a likely cause, untested: darktable **clamps each band's force to 0…1** (`CurveDataSample` with `m_max_y = 1.0`), and LightBench's `denoiseCurves` does not. With Luminance 80 / Detail 30, LightBench's finest luminance band gets 1.056, which darktable would cut to 1.0 [code-read].
   - **Capture sharpening** costs about 81 texture reads per pixel per half-iteration. darktable's kernel is a Gaussian truncated to a disc, which is nearly separable, so two 9-tap passes could replace the 9×9 gather [code-read; the speed gain is an estimate].
   - Both fixes are free of process-version cost if they land before Part 13 merges.
2. **Measure on real GPUs before optimising.** Every speed figure in PHASE3 comes from SwiftShader on a shared CPU (e.g. Part 13: "about 5.3 s per iteration"). A small benchmark harness using WebGPU timestamp queries, run on the owner's RTX 4000 Ada and an integrated GPU, is the first item.
3. **Sony CA over-correction** (Part 9) can't be fixed by tuning the table. A **raw-domain CA correction before demosaic** (darktable's `cacorrect.c`, Martinec/Weyrich, GPL-3.0; it fits a 2-D polynomial, so it also handles the A7 II's non-radial CA) is the published route. It differs from darktable's lens module, so it should be a new option.
4. **Colour Mixer** (planned): darktable's RBF band falloff dips to −14 % about 62° away ([doc] COLOUR_MIXER_EFFECTS.md §A.2). A periodic, non-overshooting interpolation, like the Tone Curve's Fritsch–Carlson fix, removes the dip at build time, with no process version.
5. **Remove on brick** got slightly worse after PatchMatch was dropped. The obvious fix (a coherence term) is in **patent-dense territory** (Adobe US 9,025,822, Yeda US 8,542,908, claims not read). The AI route (MI-GAN as a fourth mode) avoids PatchMatch-type search altogether. **CONFLICT: IQ vs patent caution.**
6. **AI, own weights.** The best case for licence freedom is **raw denoise**. The RawNIND photos are CC BY 4.0 / CC0 [secondary: darktable-ai card], and its released weights are "dual licensed GPL3 + CC-BY-4.0" [read]. A UtNet-class model trained from scratch on that data could carry MIT/Apache weights. On the owner's GPU this is about 1–2 days per training run [estimate].
7. **Inpainting.** Own weights help provenance, not the licence, since MI-GAN's weights are already MIT. Places365's images belong to their owners [read]. LaMa's training recipe uses an ADE20K-trained perceptual network (`resnet_pl`, weight 30) [read]; ADE20K is non-commercial [doc]. **Open Images V7** (images listed as CC BY 2.0 "with no representations or warranties", annotations CC BY 4.0 [read]) is the main permissive replacement.
8. **Segmentation and depth are the hard cases.** SAM-class training from scratch is far beyond one GPU, and fine-tuning SAM 2.1 keeps its SA-1B provenance. Depth can be distilled from DA-V2 Small (Apache-2.0) or trained on synthetic data (BlendedMVS is CC BY 4.0 [read]; Hypersim is CC BY-SA 3.0 [read]). **CONFLICT: licence purity vs quality** wherever a pretrained backbone is needed.
9. **Auto-tone.** There are no licence-clean public training data: MIT-Adobe FiveK is research-only [doc, AI_MODELS.md]. Options: the owner's own edits, or synthetic degradation. Compute is trivial; the data is the limit.
10. **Credit.** GPL §5 change notices in each translated file, Apache §4 NOTICE handling, MIT copyright lines, and a model card per trained model with CC BY attribution (§4).

**Notes on the table**
- **C12 lead, in numbers** [code-read]. With Luminance 80, Detail 30, Contrast 20, Part 13's mapping gives the finest Y0 band 0.8 × 2^(0.4 × 1) = 1.056, before the Contrast factor, which is 1 at the finest band. darktable samples its 7-point curve at the node positions (`res = 1/(m_samplingRes − 1)`, `curve_tools.c` 702) and clamps to 1.0. The threshold multiplier is (2·force)², so the finest band's threshold would be (1.056/1.0)² ≈ 1.115× darktable's. This is in the direction of "LightBench removes more". Whether it accounts for all of the 4–9 %, and why the X-T4's R channel differs too, needs the re-run.
- **darktable's wrap-around points** in `commit_params` (2950-2957) write anchors 0 and BANDS+1, but anchor 0 is then overwritten by the loop and `m_numAnchors` stays 7, so the extra anchor is ignored [code-read]. No effect on the comparison; noted so nobody chases it.

### 2.2 Notes per model

**A1 Raw denoise.**
- **Do first** (cost: one message): the draft question to the RawNIND author in `research-p4/GROUNDWORK.md` §3. If "GPL3 + CC-BY-4.0" means either licence may be chosen, RawNIND's own weights can be used under CC BY 4.0 now, and own training becomes an improvement project, not a licence necessity.
- **What "improve" can mean here:**
  - a smaller model for integrated GPUs (UtNet with funit 16–24, or NAFNet blocks);
  - **fp16-safe** activations, because darktable keeps the Bayer model off FP16 on CoreML for overflow [doc]: train with tone-compressed inputs, or clamp inside the net;
  - an **X-Trans-native** variant (today the linear variant serves X-Trans [doc]);
  - noise-profile conditioning: feed darktable's per-camera (a, b) profile as an input map, so one model generalises across ISO and cameras [idea].
- **12 GB fit, in numbers** [estimate]: a funit-32 U-Net at 512² output keeps about a dozen 32–256-channel feature maps. That is a few hundred MB per sample in fp16, so batch 8–16 fits.
- **Cost basis** [estimate]: about 1.1 TMAC per 24 MP Bayer frame [doc] → about 23 GFLOP forward per 256² Bayer crop → about 70 GFLOP per training sample.
- **Basis for "per run"**: RawNIND's released checkpoints are at iteration 4,350,000 (Bayer) and 3,900,000 (linear) [read, darktable-ai card]; their batch size wasn't found [unconfirmed]. 4–8 M crop-samples is a working budget, not a reproduction of theirs.

**A2 Inpainting.**
- **Train at 256², then fine-tune at 512².** This cuts compute about 4× for most of the run [estimate].
- **The perceptual loss is a licence trap.** LaMa's `resnet_pl` is an ADE20K-trained ResNet50 [read config; ADE20K NC per doc]. Options:
  - train our own feature network on Open Images classification labels (CC BY 4.0 annotations);
  - use DINOv2 (Apache-2.0 weights; its training images aren't released [unconfirmed]);
  - drop the perceptual term and keep L1 + adversarial + feature matching (lower quality [unconfirmed]).
- **CONFLICT: licence purity vs IQ.**
- **Shared domain issue** for both uses: the models are trained on 8-bit display images, so LightBench feeds them a tone-mapped crop [doc PHASE4_MODELS §1]. Training on **tone-mapped linear crops** from RawNIND clean frames, rendered through LightBench's own sigmoid, would match the input domain better [idea].

**A3 Segmentation.**
- **Realistic plan:** keep SAM 2.1 Tiny for click and hover (Apache-2.0 weights; the owner's training-data policy decides). Train **only a small one-click model** of our own (sky, subject, background) on Open Images masks plus SA-V frames.
- **Pseudo-labels:** distilling from SAM 2.1 or BiRefNet would import their provenance. That is the same policy question as using them directly.
- **Sky:** no small, clearly licensed sky model was found [doc AI_MODELS.md]. That gap is the main value of our own model.

**A4 Depth.**
- Own training mainly buys **speed** (a smaller conv student for integrated GPUs) and optionally **provenance** (synthetic-only route). The licence already passes with DA-V2 Small.
- **DA3-SMALL** (0.08 B) and **DA3MONO-LARGE** (0.35 B, with sky segmentation) are Apache 2.0 [read], so they are candidate teachers too.

**A5–A6 Demosaic and super-resolution.**
- **CONFLICT: IQ vs browser speed.** An AI demosaic at about 1–2 MFLOP per pixel [estimate] is about 24–48 TFLOP per 24 MP image. At the 3–4 TMAC/s measured for hand-written WGSL on an M5 Pro [doc, pmndrs] that is **several seconds**, versus RCD's interactive speed. It must be a **cached, per-photo step** (like AI denoise), not the live demosaic. A joint demosaic-denoise model (RawNIND's Bayer model already is one [doc]) gives better value than a separate AI demosaic.

**A7 Auto-tone.**
- **The data is the project**: about 200–500 of the owner's edited RAWs, with their LightBench slider values as targets [idea].
- **Model:** predict the six Auto sliders from LightBench's own statistics (histogram, region stats once masks exist), so results stay editable sliders (owner's Phase 4 requirement) [doc PLAN].
- **Synthetic alternative:** perturb well-exposed photos (exposure, contrast, white points) and learn to undo the perturbation. That teaches correction, not taste [idea].
